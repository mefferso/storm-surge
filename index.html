Which hurricane will flood more?

Onshore winds transfer momentum to the water. In this model, pressure makes a smaller contribution. Dashed gold marks the predicted tide and cyan marks storm tide. Breaking waves add a small setup above it. Short crests and a thin wash up the shore show wave motion and runup.

Educator guide

Ask students to predict what will happen before changing a control. Start with Category Isn’t Everything in Hurricane Showdown, then ask students to move the depth probe from the yard to Shell Ridge.

Storm surge
The storm-driven rise above the predicted astronomical tide.
Storm tide
Predicted astronomical tide plus storm surge. In the lab, the small contribution from breaking-wave setup is displayed separately.
Flood depth
Water above the ground at the probe. At a house, the floor is a separate threshold. All heights use an invented zero near everyday sea level.
Waves
Breaking waves can raise the average water level a little (setup). Individual waves can rush farther uphill (runup). The short moving crests and foam are illustrative. The depth probe measures average water.
Rain and levees
Rainwater can pool behind a levee. Overtopping starts when coastal water rises above the crest. The levee remains intact in this simulation.

The terrain, levee and water levels are invented teaching examples. Use official NHC and local NWS products for real storms.
